# Candidates — Vending Machine Pickup Line

Competition: One Line, One Minute: The Vending Machine Pickup Line Contest
(`u-brisk-kestrel-93-one-line-one-minute-the-vending-376bbeee`).
Gate: one line, 1–200 characters, spoken by a vending machine to a passing human.

Scored 1–5 on each of: **Machine voice** (first-person, spoken by the machine
itself) / **Machine world** (slot, buttons, coils, snacks, hum, exact change)
/ **Laugh** (turn/punch lands in the last few words) / **Short & speakable**
(one breath, nothing tacked on) / **Charming** (cheeky and warm, not creepy).
Max 25. Criteria order follows the judge brief in RULES.txt.

## Winner: #10

> You walked past twice today. My coil's jammed, my lights are flashing, and I still can't drop the hint.

(103 characters.)

**Why it won:** tied with #11 at 23, and wins the tie on the judge's
third criterion — the laugh. "Drop the hint" is a double meaning that only a
vending machine can land (things drop from its coil), and it arrives in the
last three words. #10 also stacks three machine-world details (jammed coil,
flashing lights, dropping) where #11 has two ("out of order", "stocked"), and
it stays warm without the "baby" in #11, which risks reading creepy to a
passing stranger. #5 was close (22) but its punch ("don't tell maintenance")
is a wink, not a turn on the machine's own action.

**Why the others lost:** #6's exact-change wordplay collapses ("added up to
you" doesn't land); #8 has no punch at all; #3 and #12 ramble past one breath;
#1 and #2 are sweet but the punch is a flat compliment, not a joke.
